How it works

What Alfie does in a week

Here's how the HubSpot work moves off your team and onto Alfie: where the jobs come from, what Alfie does on its own, what waits for your admin, and how the first weeks go.

{% include cta.html %}See what core Alfie does

From "someone should fix that" to done

  1. 01

    Alfie finds the work

    Some jobs come from your rules: Alfie checks your records and spots the duplicate pair, the deal at Proposal with no close date, the 40 new contacts with no owner. Some come from your team in Slack: "Move Acme to Sarah," or a pasted list of 60 records to update.

    ExampleFound 3 duplicate company pairs and 6 deals at Proposal missing a close date.

  2. 02

    It prepares the fix

    AI drafts each change from the request and your HubSpot data. Fixed checks written from your rules decide whether it's allowed. If it fails a rule, nothing is written and Alfie says which rule stopped it.

    ExampleTerritory passes. Acme is owned by Jordan today.

  3. 03

    It goes ahead, or waits for the right person

    Rules your admin has signed off run on their own. Merges, and anything your rules reserve for a person, wait for that person in Slack. Rule 1.1, for example, sends account moves to Dana, the sales lead.

    ExampleRule 1.1: moving an account to a different rep needs the sales lead. @Dana

  4. 04

    It makes the change and checks it stuck

    Alfie writes to HubSpot, then reads the record back by ID. It isn't marked done until HubSpot matches. Each change goes into a record you can export.

    ExampleAcme assigned to Sarah. Confirmed in HubSpot.

You stay in control

How the first weeks go

  1. 01

    First week: Alfie only reads

    It checks your records against the rules and proposes fixes, but writes nothing yet. You get a baseline of where your data stands.

  2. 02

    Then: you OK the fixes

    Alfie starts clearing the backlog, and each change waits for a yes in Slack.

  3. 03

    Later: trusted rules run on their own

    When your admin is happy with how a rule behaves, they sign it off and Alfie stops asking. The sign-off is recorded against that version of the rule. Merges always wait for your admin, however long you've used Alfie.

The rules live in Alfie. You get them in writing.

Your rules of engagement cover who owns what, which fields each stage needs, and how duplicates are matched. You get a written copy as Markdown or PDF, and a new version whenever a rule changes or a process is added.

What Alfie takes on first

We start with the jobs that pile up in almost every growing team's HubSpot. Alfie finds duplicates and drafts the merges. It standardizes field values and nudges the owners of deals that reach a stage without the details your team agreed, in a Slack digest, with the exceptions listed for your admin. It suggests which companies belong under a parent, like Acme EU under Acme, from the fields already in HubSpot. And it routes new contacts and companies to the right owner by account, territory or segment, and round robin, with your admin approving. After that, you add the next process when you're ready.

By the end of month one, you should have:

  • A backlog of duplicates and missing fields worked through, with a before-and-after view
  • New contacts and companies going to the right owner without anyone sorting them
  • Rules for clean data, required fields and routing, in writing

Some processes are custom, or nobody ever pinned them down. Services takes those on.
