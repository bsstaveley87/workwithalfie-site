## What does a RevOps person actually do?

At a growing SaaS company, a first RevOps hire usually covers three kinds of work:

- **Strategy.** Pipeline definitions, forecasting process, territory design, comp plan mechanics, and the reporting the leadership team runs the business on.
- **Systems.** The CRM and everything connected to it: marketing tools, billing, product data, support. They decide how data moves between them.
- **Upkeep.** Duplicates, missing fields, ownership changes, imports, the steady stream of "can you fix this record" requests.

The first two need judgment, context and time in meetings. The third is necessary, repetitive, and the part that most often lands on a founder or a sales lead who never asked for it.

## What does Alfie cover?

Alfie is a RevOps service for growing SaaS companies that run on HubSpot: hands-on RevOps help plus automation that does the repetitive HubSpot upkeep, like merging duplicates, fixing required fields and routing new contacts and companies, by rules you set and with your admin in control of what changes.

In practice, that means the upkeep layer, run on written rules:

- **Clean records.** Duplicates found by rule and merged once your admin approves, fields standardized, and deals flagged when they reach a stage without the details your team agreed.
- **Parent and child companies.** Alfie uses AI to suggest parent and subsidiary company links, and nothing changes in HubSpot until you approve it.
- **Record updates.** Your team asks for a change in Slack, one record or a pasted list, and Alfie checks it against your rules before it reaches HubSpot.
- **Your rules, in writing.** Alfie keeps your approved rules and gives you a written copy, your rules of engagement, as Markdown or PDF. It's yours whether or not you keep using Alfie.

Account ownership is a later process, added once clean data is running. For processes that are custom or were never defined, [Services](/services/) is the people side: we work out how the process really runs today, design the fix with you, document the rules, and then Alfie runs it.

From day one, Alfie checks your HubSpot against the rules you approved and proposes fixes. In the first week it only reads. Changes go live once a person approves them, and a rule runs on its own only after your admin signs it off. See [how the first weeks work](/how-it-works/).

## When is a RevOps hire the better choice?

Be honest with yourself here. A hire is the better fit if:

- **You need strategy more than upkeep.** If the open questions are "how should we segment the market" or "how should commission work", you need a person with judgment and a seat at the table.
- **Your stack is wide.** If RevOps means stitching HubSpot to billing, product usage and a data warehouse, and making decisions across all of them, that's a job for someone in the business every day.
- **You already have the rules and the volume.** If your rules are written, your team is large, and the upkeep alone fills a role, a dedicated person may be the right investment.
- **You're on a CRM other than HubSpot.** Alfie works with HubSpot only.

If you already have a RevOps team with your rules written down and running, you probably don't need Alfie.

## When does ongoing help make more sense?

It tends to fit when:

- **HubSpot is still on the founder's desk.** You've run it since the first deal, and the upkeep now competes with selling and hiring.
- **Someone inherited it.** A sales, marketing or ops lead picked HubSpot up along the way and is fixing records on top of the day job.
- **You're not sure what the role should be yet.** Running the basics on written rules first shows you what's left. That makes the eventual job description sharper.
- **The rules live in someone's head.** Writing them down and having them checked is useful whether or not you hire later.

## Can you have both?

Yes, and it's a common path. If you already have an early RevOps hire, Alfie works as their second pair of hands: they set the rules, and Alfie runs them. Their time goes to the next process instead of merging duplicates and fixing owners.

If you hire later, the person starts with a written rules document, a record of what's changed and why, and a HubSpot setup that has been kept in step. That's a better first week than inheriting a portal nobody has documented.

## What should you ask before deciding?

Five questions help:

1. **What's the work, really?** List the last month of HubSpot requests. Count how many were upkeep and how many needed a real decision.
2. **Who owns the rules today?** If the answer is "nobody" or "it's in my head", start there, whichever route you take.
3. **How many systems are involved?** One CRM and Slack is a different job from five connected tools.
4. **Who approves changes?** Decide who signs off on merges, reassignments and exceptions. Both a hire and Alfie need that answer.
5. **What would a hire do in their first 90 days?** If the honest answer is "clean up", consider getting the cleanup moving first.
