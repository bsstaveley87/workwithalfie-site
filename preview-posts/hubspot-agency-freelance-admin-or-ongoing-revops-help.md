## Why is this choice harder than it looks?

Because all three can do the same task. Any of them can merge duplicates, fix a pipeline or build a report. The difference is in how the work is shaped: a project with a scope and an end date, a person's hours, or a standing process that runs on rules.

Most growing SaaS teams don't need to pick one forever. The question is which shape fits the problem in front of you now. HubSpot itself is rarely the issue. What usually needs work is the setup, and keeping it in step as the team changes.

## When is a HubSpot agency the right call?

Agencies are built around projects, and that's their strength. Consider one when:

- **You're setting HubSpot up, or rebuilding it.** A new portal, a pipeline redesign or a move from another CRM is a project with a start and an end.
- **You need a range of skills at once.** Marketing automation, website, integrations and reporting in one engagement.
- **You want a defined deliverable.** A scope, a timeline and a handover.

What to check: who keeps the setup running once the project ends, and whether the rules behind it are written down for your team to keep.

## When is a freelance admin the right call?

A good freelance HubSpot admin is flexible, hands-on and often deeply experienced. Consider one when:

- **The work changes week to week.** A report one week, a property cleanup the next, a new form after that.
- **You want one named person.** Someone who learns your portal and your team.
- **Volume is modest.** A few hours here and there covers it.

What to check: what happens when they're busy or move on, and whether the decisions they make are written down somewhere your team can see.

## When does ongoing RevOps help make more sense?

Ongoing help fits when the work is steady and repeats: duplicates keep coming back, deals reach stages without the details your team agreed, and people keep asking for record changes. That work doesn't end, so a project doesn't fit it well, and it's the kind of work that's easy to get done slightly differently every time.

This is where Alfie sits. Alfie is a RevOps service for growing SaaS companies that run on HubSpot: hands-on RevOps help plus automation that does the repetitive HubSpot upkeep, like merging duplicates, fixing required fields and routing new contacts and companies, by rules you set and with your admin in control of what changes.

The difference is the starting point. Alfie starts from written rules you approve, checks your records against them, asks the right person in Slack when a rule says so, and keeps a record of every change. You keep the rules document whether or not you keep using Alfie.

## How do you decide which you need?

Start with the work, not the provider. Four questions help:

1. **Does it end?** If the work has a finish line, it's a project. If it comes back every week, it's a process.
2. **Is it the same each time?** Repeating work benefits from written rules. One-off judgment calls benefit from a person.
3. **Who should approve changes?** If merges, reassignments and exceptions need sign-off, decide who signs off and make sure whoever does the work asks them.
4. **What do you keep at the end?** Whichever route you choose, ask for the rules and the record of what changed. That's what lets you switch, hire or scale without starting over.

## Can you combine them?

Often, yes. A common pattern is an agency or freelance admin for a rebuild or a specific project, then ongoing help to keep the result in step. If you already work with a freelance admin you trust, written rules and a record of changes make their work easier to hand over, too.

If the setup itself needs a rethink before anything can run on rules, say so on a first call. For processes that are custom or were never defined, Services works out how the process really runs today before Alfie runs it.
