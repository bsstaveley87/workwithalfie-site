## What does a RevOps person actually do?

At a growing SaaS company, a first RevOps hire usually covers three kinds of work:

- **Strategy.** Pipeline definitions, forecasting process, territory design, comp plan mechanics, and the reporting the leadership team runs the business on.
- **Systems.** The CRM and everything connected to it: marketing tools, billing, product data, support. They decide how data moves between them.
- **Upkeep.** Merging the two "Acme Inc" records, chasing the deal at Proposal with no close date, moving a leaver's accounts to new owners, and the steady stream of "can you fix this record" requests.

The first two need judgment, context and time in meetings. The third never stops coming, and it usually lands on a founder or a sales lead who never asked for it.

## What does Alfie cover?

Alfie does the third kind of work, the repetitive HubSpot upkeep, so nobody on your team has to.

Alfie is a RevOps service for growing SaaS companies that run on HubSpot: hands-on RevOps help plus automation that does the repetitive HubSpot upkeep, like merging duplicates, fixing {% include post-link.html slug="hubspot-required-fields-by-deal-stage" text="required fields" %} and routing new contacts and companies, by rules you set and with your admin in control of what changes.

In practice, these are the jobs it takes on:

- **Merges duplicates.** Alfie finds {% include post-link.html slug="merge-duplicate-companies-hubspot" text="duplicate contacts and companies" %} by your matching rules, like the two Acme Incs, and drafts each merge with the surviving values chosen by rule.
- **Fills and fixes required fields.** It catches deals that reach a stage without what your team agreed, like a close date at Proposal, and sends each owner a Slack digest of what's missing.
- **Routes new contacts and companies.** Alfie routes new contacts and companies to the right owner by account, territory or segment, and round robin, with your admin approving.
- **Moves a leaver's accounts.** When a rep leaves, Alfie proposes who takes over each account, and nothing moves until your admin approves.
- **Tidies properties.** It standardizes emails, phone numbers, websites, countries and states, and clears placeholder values like 'test' or 'n/a'.
- **Links parents and subsidiaries.** AI suggests which companies belong together, like Acme EU under Acme, from the fields already in HubSpot, and you confirm before anything is linked.
- **Takes record updates from Slack.** Your team asks for a change, one record or a pasted list, and Alfie makes it once it passes your rules.

Example: Priya, a rep, leaves on a Friday with 300 accounts still in her name. By Monday, Alfie has proposed an owner for each one, and Sam, your admin, clears the list from one Slack message instead of an afternoon in HubSpot views.

We start on standard rules for the basics, adjusted to your team, and you get a written copy of them, your rules of engagement, as Markdown or PDF. It's yours whether or not you keep using Alfie. ## When is a RevOps hire the better choice?

Be honest with yourself here. A hire is the better fit if:

- **You need strategy more than upkeep.** If the open questions are "how should we segment the market" or "how should commission work", you need a person with judgment and a seat at the table.
- **Your stack is wide.** If RevOps means stitching HubSpot to billing, product usage and a data warehouse, and making decisions across all of them, that's a job for someone in the business every day.
- **You already have the rules and the volume.** If your rules are written, your team is large, and the upkeep alone fills a role, a dedicated person may be the right investment.
- **You're on a CRM other than HubSpot.** Alfie works with HubSpot only.

If you already have a RevOps team with your rules written down and running, you probably don't need Alfie.

## When does ongoing help make more sense?

It tends to fit when:

- **HubSpot is still on the founder's desk.** You've run it since the first deal, and you're still the one merging records at 11 PM.
- **Someone inherited it.** A sales, marketing or ops lead picked HubSpot up along the way and is fixing records on top of the day job, with a cleanup list that never gets shorter.
- **You're not sure what the role should be yet.** Handing off the upkeep first shows you what's left. That makes the eventual job description sharper.
- **The rules live in someone's head.** "Enterprise deals go to Dana" works until someone new has to apply it. Writing the rules down is useful whether or not you hire later.

## Can you have both?

Yes, and it's a common path. If you already have an early RevOps hire, Alfie works as their second pair of hands: they set the rules, and Alfie runs them. Their time goes to the next process instead of merging duplicates and reassigning a leaver's accounts.

If you hire later, the person starts with a written rules document, a record of each change Alfie made, and a HubSpot setup that has been kept in step. That's a better first week than inheriting a portal nobody has documented.

## What should you ask before deciding?

Five questions help:

1. **What's the work, really?** List the last month of HubSpot requests. Count how many were upkeep and how many needed a real decision.
2. **Who owns the rules today?** If the answer is "nobody" or "it's in my head", start there, whichever route you take.
3. **How many systems are involved?** One CRM and Slack is a different job from five connected tools.
4. **Who has the final say on changes?** Decide who OKs merges, account moves and exceptions. A hire and Alfie both need that settled.
5. **What would a hire do in their first 90 days?** If the honest answer is "clean up", consider getting the cleanup moving first.
